# Video speed comparison

Official sources checked 2026-10-30. Public numerical data is also in [comparison.json](comparison.json).

**Our measured warm median is 9.50 seconds per audiovisual task on one RTX 5090.** The first-task median is 25.11 seconds. The workload is 640x384, 107 frames, 24 FPS and eight steps. Four warm samples and two first samples are recorded in [reference.json](reference.json). These task clocks include processing and saving; process startup is separate. Repeated conditioning was warm.

The full coordinator failed its process-identity guard before final exit. Twelve completed saved audiovisual outputs passed a separate full-decode and same-profile repeatability audit. Those saved results remain useful measurements; the overall execution remains failed. This is a historical development-stack result, not a fresh GPU test of the public export, a quality comparison, or a measured gain attributable to every patch in this package.

**There is no defensible fastest-engine ranking from these sources.** Some publishers report lower numbers on different models, output settings or hardware. Our engine keeps stored model weights unchanged; that does not make its pre-existing model/precision recipe identical to another model.

## What a direct speed test would require

Match the exact model/revision and weight representation; input conditioning and cache state; resolution, frame count, FPS, steps and sampler; attention/decoder/precision; native audio; GPU count and memory/offload. Report cold startup, first task, warm task, denoising and completed-file time separately. Check decoded output completeness and quality independently. Preserve failed runs. Until that matched test exists, the tables support candidate selection and source inspection, not a claim that this package beats every local engine or frontier service.
